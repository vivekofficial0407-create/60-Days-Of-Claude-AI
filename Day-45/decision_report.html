The Real Decision

This isn't really "which field pays more" — it's whether to trust your genuine interest and existing momentum in AI/ML, or hedge against FOMO by chasing broader job-market safety.

The trade-off: depth in one domain (interview-ready, portfolio-strong) vs. spreading thin across three to avoid "choosing wrong."

It's emotionally hard because the fear isn't about the work — it's about wasting 2–3 irreplaceable years on the wrong bet.

The Case For Each Option

Option A — Data Science

Strongest case: High demand across industries, strong analytical foundation that transfers well to many roles.

Hidden upside: DS roles often serve as a stepping stone into ML roles later, since the stats/foundations overlap.

Biggest weakness: You didn't mention genuine interest here — it's a "safe demand" pick, not a "pull" pick.

Best if you value: broad demand and analytical variety over building intelligent systems.

Option B — AI / ML Engineering

Strongest case: You're already building AI-powered projects and genuinely enjoy the work — momentum plus interest is rare and valuable.

Hidden upside: Building real AI projects now compounds into a differentiated portfolio + YouTube content flywheel — few peers will have both.

Biggest weakness: Narrower entry-level hiring pool than full-stack; interviews tend to be more technically demanding.

Best if you value: long-term alignment, deep technical mastery, and building a distinct personal brand.

Option C — Full-Stack Development

Strongest case: More job openings, generally faster and more predictable path to a first internship/placement.

Hidden upside: Full-stack skills make you self-sufficient to ship your own AI products end-to-end later.

Biggest weakness: Least aligned with your stated interest and current project direction — you'd be learning it mostly for "safety," not pull.

Best if you value: job-market breadth and speed-to-placement over specialization.

Assumption Buster

3 assumptions you may be making:

  • That "more job openings" (Full-Stack) automatically means better odds for you specifically, regardless of fit or interest.
  • That choosing AI/ML means closing the door on Data Science or Full-Stack entirely, rather than them being adjacent skill sets.
  • That the "right" choice is knowable in advance, rather than something proven through 2 weeks of focused testing.

2 biases at play:

  • FOMO / status quo bias: weighing "what everyone else might be doing" over your own already-validated interest and traction.
  • Loss aversion: the fear of "wasting time" is looming larger than the upside of committing to what you already enjoy and have started.

1 thing you're definitely ignoring:

You already have a head start in AI/ML (existing projects) — switching away from that discards real accumulated progress, not just a preference.
